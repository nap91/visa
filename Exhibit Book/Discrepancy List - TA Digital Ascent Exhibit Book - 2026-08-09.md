# Discrepancy List
## TA Digital Ascent Exhibit Book — Navdeep Pandey
Compiled 9 August 2026

This list records everything found in the source files that contradicts, complicates or fails
to support what an exhibit was expected to show. It is ordered by how much it matters, not by
exhibit number. Every item was read from the source file itself.

---

### 1. The internal initiative you are named as leading is not the Ascent initiative

Exhibit 3. The June 2023 Data Practice deck contains two tables. In "Ongoing Internal
Initiatives" your name appears once, against **"Recipes/Prescriptions for analytics dashboards
& Calculated Metrics"**, ETA Q3, with the comment "Standardize calculated metrics, report
collections (universal, business unit specific, etc.)."

Two pages later, the "New Internal Initiatives" table contains the row **"Recreate Ascent
keeping data & industry in mind"** — and the Led By column reads **TBD**.

So the deck that names you as an initiative owner also shows, on an adjacent page, that as of
June 2023 the Ascent initiative had no owner assigned and was not assigned to you. Anyone
reading page 9 will read page 10. I have reproduced page 10 inside Exhibit 3 rather than leave
it to be found.

This does not sink the exhibit — it still proves you owned a named company initiative — but the
exhibit cannot be described as showing Ascent ownership, and the cover sheet does not describe
it that way.

### 2. There is no "six areas of specialization" list in the Ascent role document

Exhibit 8. The brief asked for "the passage listing the six areas of specialization." No such
passage exists. The word *specialization*, and every variant of it, appears **zero times** in
the six-page document.

What exists is one sentence on page 1: the beneficiary "has a high level of expertise in the
overall project architecture, solution design, system integration documentation, and developing
project technology strategy by understanding key client objectives, diagnosing, and mapping
client requirements; articulating solution risks and barriers; recommending project approaches;
preparing time and cost estimates; planning entire technical project life cycle."

That is a single run-on sentence, not an enumerated list, and it does not contain the phrase
"data processing" which a prior summary attributed to it. Any document that cites "six areas of
specialization" as though the petition record contains such a list is citing something that
isn't there. If that phrasing has already gone to counsel, it should be corrected.

I substituted the page 1 definition of Ascent and its named components, which is stronger
evidence anyway, and the cover sheet states plainly that no specialization list exists.

### 3. Slide 15 of Practice_Structure.pptx is a hidden slide

Exhibit 5. The "A&P Structure" slide carries `show="0"` in the PowerPoint file, meaning it is
skipped when the deck is presented. It did not appear at all in a normal PDF conversion; I had
to enable export of hidden slides to render it.

That matters because the slide is a *proposed re-designation* chart — the footnote legend reads
"Currently 'Technical Architect'", i.e. the titles shown were not yet in effect. A hidden,
forward-looking slide is weaker than it appears, and if the company is asked to authenticate it
they may describe it as a draft. State it as a draft or leave it out; do not present it as the
org chart.

The same slide does contain two things worth having: you report to Stetson Barnhouse, Principal
Architect, and two people (Akash Awasthi, Abhishek Agarwal) sit below you.

### 7. The GCOM statement of work contradicts itself on the client name

Exhibit 12. The body names the client as "GCOM Partners". The footer on every page reads
**"JCI Supplier Diversity Empowerment"**. That is a different entity name on the face of the
